01Key findings

Borrowing makes it worse. At 7%, the deal doesn’t pencil.

76110 Fort Worth, my pick from 166 ranked ZIPs, returns less with a mortgage than without one.

1.1%

Levered IRR, below the 5.0% all-cash return

The asset yields about 4.3% unlevered but the debt costs 7%. Financing cuts the 10-year IRR from 5.0% to 1.1%. Equity multiple: 1.10x.

–$3,718

Year-1 cash flow, negative all ten years

Still –$877 in Year 10. Cash-on-cash is –6.1% in Year 1, and the return relies entirely on appreciation at exit.

~52%

Property tax as a share of NOI

At the 2.24% non-homestead rate, Year-1 tax is $4,878: the largest operating expense (46% of OpEx) and the biggest single reason a 9.6% gross yield becomes a 4.3% cap rate.

~$2,144

Monthly rent needed to break even

About 23% above today’s $1,744. Exiting at the 4.3% entry cap instead of 5.0% lifts levered IRR to only ~5.1%.

04How the model works

Named inputs, one linked pro forma

  1. Load and reshape the data

    Power Query pulls Zillow’s national ZHVI and ZORI CSVs, filters to Dallas–Fort Worth–Arlington and unpivots the monthly columns. ZIPs stay as text.

  2. Screen and select

    Rank 166 ZIPs on yield (70%) and 3-year rent CAGR (30%), then override the yield trap and pick 76110.

  3. Amortize the loan

    A 360-month schedule on a $163,317 loan at 7% pays down to $0; the Year-10 balance feeds the exit.

  4. Build the 10-year pro forma

    Gross rent → vacancy → EGI → five escalating expenses → NOI → debt service → cash flow, with a terminal-cap exit net of 7% selling costs.

  5. Stress test

    Two live Excel Data Tables (exit cap × rent growth, price × rate) and a Goal Seek for breakeven rent.

05Sensitivity

What would have to be true for it to work

06Verdict

Pass at 7%. A disciplined, evidence-backed no.

76110 was my pick after skipping three likely yield traps, and it still doesn’t clear the bar. At today’s price and rate it delivers roughly a 1% IRR, below risk-free Treasuries, in exchange for leverage, tenant and vacancy risk, illiquidity, and a decade of out-of-pocket carry.

End-to-end build

Raw public data → automated ETL → quantitative screen → underwriting model → stress testing → a written recommendation.

Return math

Cap rate, NOI, DSCR, cash-on-cash, levered and unlevered IRR, and equity multiple over a 10-year hold.

Judgment over mechanics

Spotting a yield trap, using 3% forward rent growth instead of the recent 6.2% spike, and reaching an honest pass.
