01Key findings

Borrowing makes it worse. At 7%, the deal doesn’t pencil.

76110 Fort Worth is the strongest risk-adjusted pick of 166 ranked ZIPs, and it still returns less with a mortgage than without one.

1.1% vs 5.0%

Negative leverage

The asset yields about 4.3% unlevered, but the debt costs 7%. Financing it cuts the 10-year IRR from 5.0% all-cash to 1.1% levered.

0.71 → 0.93

The mortgage is never covered

DSCR stays below 1.0 for all ten years. Lenders typically require 1.20 or more, so this wouldn’t qualify for standard investment financing.

~52% of NOI

Texas property-tax drag

At the full 2.24% non-homestead rate, Year-1 tax is $4,878: the largest expense (46% of OpEx) and the main reason a 9.6% gross yield shrinks to a 4.3% cap rate.

~$2,144/mo

Breakeven rent, 23% above today

Year-1 cash flow reaches zero only at about $2,144/mo, versus $1,744 actual. Exiting at the entry cap instead of 5.0% lifts levered IRR to only ~5.1%.

05Verdict

Pass at 7%. A disciplined, evidence-backed no.

76110 is the strongest conventional value the screen surfaced in DFW, and it still doesn’t clear the bar. At today’s price and rate it delivers roughly a 1% IRR, below risk-free Treasuries, in exchange for leverage, tenant and vacancy risk, illiquidity, and a decade of out-of-pocket carry.

Against an 8% levered hurdle, it becomes worth revisiting only if the price falls to ~$185K (–15%) at a 7% mortgage, or to ~$196K (–10%) if rates ease to 5–6%.

  • End-to-end buildRaw public data → automated ETL → quantitative screen → underwriting model → stress testing → a written recommendation, in one workbook.
  • Return mathCap rate, NOI, DSCR, cash-on-cash, levered and unlevered IRR, and equity multiple.
  • Judgment over mechanicsSpotting a yield trap, using 3% forward rent growth instead of the recent 6.2% spike, and reaching an honest pass.
